If someone asked about the riskiest systems

What this register could hand over about the systems it flags high-impact, if someone asked.

Suppose someone in authority asked an agency to account for its riskiest AI systems. The register has a name for those: it flags them high-impact, meaning the agency judged the system's output to be the principal basis for a decision affecting people. This page asks what the published register could hand over about them, and finds that for most it holds almost nothing.

Nobody asked. This page is a rehearsal: it takes a question an oversight body would plausibly ask and checks whether the published register could answer it. No agency requested this, and no agency has been asked to respond to it.

The classification field has four states, not two

Entries are not simply serious or not serious. The field has four states, and one of them is that nothing was recorded, which is not a judgment about the system at all.

High-impact means the agency judged the system's output to be the principal basis for a decision affecting people. The table below shows how many entries carry each state, in the wording the data itself uses. Selecting a group opens the register filtered to those entries.

Oversight information nearly always arrives whole, or not at all

Nine fields cover human oversight, filled in only for high-impact entries. Almost every such entry has all nine filled or all nine empty. Two out of hundreds sit in between.

The nine ask whether the system was tested before deployment, whether an impact assessment was completed, what the potential impacts are, whether an independent review took place, whether performance is monitored, whether operators are trained, whether there is a fail-safe, whether a person can appeal, and whether affected groups were consulted.
